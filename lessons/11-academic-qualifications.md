# Aeraf CV – Resume Writer Training Programme
## Day 11 – Writing the Education & Academic Qualifications Section

**Module:** Week 2 – Resume Sections  
**Duration:** 10–15 minutes

### 🎯 Learning Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Present academic qualifications in a professional format.
- Decide which educational details are relevant to a target job.
- Arrange qualifications in the correct order.
- Present education appropriately for freshers and experienced candidates.
- Distinguish academic qualifications from short courses and certifications.

---

### 1. Why Is the Education Section Important?

The Education section tells recruiters about a candidate's academic background and formal qualifications.

It helps employers understand whether the candidate meets the educational requirements of a role.

For example:

- A Junior Web Developer role may require a degree or diploma in Computer Science or a related field.
- An Office Administrator role may accept higher secondary education along with relevant administrative experience.
- An HR Coordinator role may prefer an HR-related qualification, but transferable experience and relevant training can also be useful.

The importance of this section varies depending on the candidate's experience and the job requirements.

### 2. Standard Format for Education Details

A typical education entry should contain:

**Qualification / Degree**  
Institution Name | Location  
Year of Completion

Optional details, when relevant:
- Specialisation or major.
- Grade, percentage or CGPA.
- Relevant academic achievements.
- Relevant coursework or subjects.

**Example:**

**Bachelor of Computer Applications (BCA)**  
Coastal Institute of Technology | Mangaluru, Karnataka  
2026

This format is clean, simple and easy to understand.

### 3. How Should We Arrange Educational Qualifications?

Generally, use reverse-chronological order: list the most recent qualification first.

**Example:**

**Bachelor of Computer Applications (BCA)**  
Coastal Institute of Technology | Mangaluru, Karnataka  
2026

**Pre-University / Higher Secondary Education**  
Institution Name | Location  
2023

**Secondary School Education**  
Institution Name | Location  
2021

The exact qualifications and dates must always be verified with the candidate.

For experienced professionals, it is usually sufficient to include the highest or most relevant qualifications. There is no need to list every school attended unless the employer requests it or it adds value.

### 4. How Much Education Detail Should We Include?

The amount of detail depends on the candidate's profile.

**For freshers:**

Education is often one of the most important resume sections.

Consider including:
- Degree or diploma.
- College or institution.
- Graduation year.
- Relevant coursework, if useful.
- Academic projects.
- Academic achievements, if verified.

**For experienced professionals:**

Keep the education section concise.

Usually include:
- Highest qualification.
- Institution.
- Completion year.
- Relevant professional qualifications, where applicable.

Avoid lengthy descriptions of school-level subjects when the candidate has substantial work experience.

**For career changers:**

Include both formal academic qualifications and relevant additional training.

For example, a candidate moving into HR may have a degree in English and a separate HR Fundamentals course. Both can be useful, but they should be presented accurately under the appropriate headings.

### 5. Should We Include Marks, Percentage or CGPA?

Not always.

Include grades or academic scores when:

- The employer specifically requests them.
- The candidate is a fresher and the results strengthen the application.
- The score is relevant to the selection criteria.
- The candidate wants to highlight a genuine academic achievement.

Avoid including low or irrelevant scores simply to fill space.

**Important:** Never convert a percentage into a CGPA, or one grading system into another, without a verified and appropriate conversion method.

If the candidate has not supplied their marks, do not estimate them.

### 6. Education vs Certifications vs Short Courses

These are different categories and should not be presented as if they are the same.

| Category | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Academic Qualification | Formal school, college or university qualification | BCA, BA, B.Com |
| Professional Certification | A recognised certification awarded after meeting specified requirements | AWS Certified Solutions Architect |
| Short Course | A training programme covering a particular subject | HR Fundamentals |
| Workshop | Short practical or introductory learning activity | Introductory React Workshop |

A short course does not automatically make someone a certified professional.

Use the exact title shown on the certificate or completion record. Include the issuing organisation and completion date where available and relevant.

### 7. Practical Examples – Our Four Candidates

Let's prepare suitable education entries using the information in our workbook.

**A. Aisha Rahman – Fresher / Junior Web Developer**

**Education**

**Bachelor of Computer Applications (BCA)**  
Coastal Institute of Technology | Mangaluru, Karnataka  
2026

**Additional Training**

Introductory React Workshop | 2025

Why this works:
- Her degree is clearly visible.
- The React workshop is presented separately from her degree.
- Her academic project can be covered in a separate Projects section.

**B. Rahul Shetty – Office Administrator**

**Education**

**Higher Secondary Certificate**  
2019

Why this works:
- It presents the qualification available in the workbook.
- His extensive administrative experience can receive greater emphasis elsewhere in the resume.

Ask Rahul for his institution name, location and any additional verified qualifications before finalising the section.

**C. Naveen Kumar – Sales Executive**

**Education**

**Pre-University Course (PUC)**  
2019

Why this works:
- His academic qualification is included without unnecessary detail.
- His retail and customer-service experience should receive greater emphasis because it is directly relevant to sales roles.

Ask Naveen for his institution name, location and any additional relevant qualifications.

**D. Priya Nair – Career Changer / HR Coordinator**

**Education**

**Bachelor of Arts (English)**  
2020

**Additional Training**

**HR Fundamentals – Short Course**  
2025 | Completion Certificate

Why this works:
- Her degree is presented accurately.
- Her HR-related training is visible and relevant to her career transition.
- The short course is not incorrectly presented as a professional HR degree or employment experience.

Ask Priya for the institution names, locations and issuing organisation for her course certificate.

*Note: The workbook does not supply every institution name or education detail for all four candidates. Do not invent missing information. Request it from the candidate before finalising the resume.*

### 8. Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Listing qualifications in random order.
2. Confusing a short course with a formal degree.
3. Writing an incorrect degree title or institution name.
4. Including unverified marks, grades or academic achievements.
5. Listing every school qualification when it adds no value.
6. Omitting a relevant qualification because the candidate has work experience.
7. Claiming a certification when the candidate only attended a workshop.
8. Using inconsistent date and formatting styles.

**Resume Writer Tip:** Always check the candidate's certificates, transcripts or other reliable records when the exact qualification title is unclear.

### 9. Short Exercise – Identify the Problems

Review this sample Education section:

**EDUCATION**

- BCA – 2026
- HR Certified Professional – 2025
- College – 2019
- 85% marks (not verified)

Identify at least four problems.

Think about:
- Missing institution names.
- Incomplete qualification details.
- Unsupported academic scores.
- Whether the HR credential is actually a professional certification or only a short course.

### 10. Small Assignment – Prepare Four Education Sections

Using the four fictional candidate profiles, prepare an Education section for each:

1. **Aisha Rahman:** BCA and Introductory React Workshop.
2. **Rahul Shetty:** Higher Secondary Certificate.
3. **Naveen Kumar:** Pre-University Course.
4. **Priya Nair:** BA English and HR Fundamentals short course.

For each candidate:

- Use a consistent format.
- Arrange entries in reverse-chronological order where applicable.
- Separate academic qualifications from additional training.
- Identify missing information that needs confirmation.
- Do not invent institution names, marks, grades or certificate details.

### Key Takeaways

- Present qualifications clearly and consistently.
- Use reverse-chronological order.
- Give education greater prominence for freshers.
- Keep education concise for experienced professionals.
- Separate degrees, certifications, short courses and workshops.
- Include grades only when relevant and verified.
- Never invent or exaggerate academic credentials.
