# Aeraf CV – Resume Writer Training Programme

## DAY 17 – USING ACTION VERBS EFFECTIVELY

**Week:** 3 – Professional Resume Writing  
**Duration:** 10–15 minutes

### 1. Learning Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
- Select appropriate action verbs for different job roles.
- Replace weak and repetitive words with stronger alternatives.
- Choose verbs that accurately reflect a candidate's contribution.
- Maintain consistent verb tense in resume bullet points.

### 2. What Are Action Verbs?

Action verbs are words that describe what a candidate actually did in their job, project or activity.

Compare:

**Weak:** Responsible for maintaining customer records.

**Stronger:** Maintained customer records and updated CRM information.

The second statement is direct, professional and easier to understand.

### 3. Action Verbs for Different Job Roles

Choose verbs based on the candidate's actual responsibilities.

| Job area | Useful action verbs |
|---|---|
| Administration | Organised, Maintained, Prepared, Coordinated |
| Sales | Generated, Presented, Negotiated, Assisted |
| Customer Service | Responded, Resolved, Supported, Guided |
| Technical | Developed, Designed, Implemented, Tested |
| Management | Led, Managed, Supervised, Directed |
| Communication | Communicated, Presented, Explained, Liaised |
| Analysis | Analysed, Evaluated, Identified, Reviewed |
| Training | Trained, Mentored, Instructed, Guided |

**Important:** A strong action verb is not necessarily a complicated word. Choose the word that most accurately describes the work.

### 4. Transform Weak Statements into Strong Statements

Let's look at some examples.

| Weak statement | Improved statement |
|---|---|
| Did office work | Prepared reports and maintained office documentation |
| Helped customers | Assisted customers with product enquiries |
| Worked on a website | Developed a responsive website using HTML and CSS |
| Looked after sales | Supported retail sales through customer engagement |
| Checked customer details | Verified customer information and updated CRM records |

Notice how the improved statements begin with specific action verbs.

### 6. Choosing the Right Verb – An Important Skill

Some verbs communicate different levels of responsibility.

For example:

- **Assisted:** The candidate supported someone else's work.
- **Coordinated:** The candidate organised activities involving people or tasks.
- **Managed:** The candidate had responsibility for managing a process or function.
- **Led:** The candidate provided direction or leadership.
- **Developed:** The candidate created or built something.
- **Implemented:** The candidate put a plan, system or solution into practice.

Never use a more impressive verb simply to make a candidate sound senior.

### 7. Verb Tense: Present vs Past

Use consistent verb tenses based on the employment period.

**Current employment:** Use present tense for ongoing responsibilities.

- Manage customer enquiries.
- Maintain office records.
- Coordinate meetings.

**Previous employment:** Use past tense.

- Managed customer enquiries.
- Maintained office records.
- Coordinated meetings.

For completed projects, use past tense even if the candidate is currently employed.

### 8. Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Starting every bullet point with the same verb.
2. Using complicated words unnecessarily.
3. Using verbs that exaggerate the candidate's responsibility.
4. Mixing present and past tense without reason.
5. Using vague verbs such as Did, Worked and Helped repeatedly.
6. Adding impressive action verbs without evidence of the actual activity.

### 9. Short Exercise

Replace the weak verbs in the following statements with appropriate action verbs.

1. Did customer service work.
2. Worked on website development.
3. Helped in organising meetings.
4. Looked after company records.
5. Was responsible for training new employees.

Try to write each statement as a professional resume bullet point.

### 10. Today's Assignment

Choose one fictional candidate from the Aeraf CV Workbook.

- Identify five existing resume bullet points or basic responsibility statements.
- Highlight the weak or repetitive verbs.
- Replace them with more appropriate action verbs.
- Ensure the new verbs accurately represent the candidate's responsibilities.
- Check that the verb tense is consistent.

**Bonus Challenge:** Identify 10 different action verbs relevant to the candidate's target job role and create a personal Action Verb Bank for future resume-writing assignments.

### 11. Key Takeaways

- Action verbs make resume bullet points clearer and stronger.
- Select verbs based on the actual job responsibilities.
- Avoid repetitive and vague wording.
- Maintain consistent verb tense.
- Strong wording must never come at the cost of accuracy.
