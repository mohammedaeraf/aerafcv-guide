## DAY 15 – TAILORING RESUMES TO SPECIFIC JOB ROLES

**Week:** 3 – Professional Resume Writing  
**Duration:** 10–15 minutes  
**Module:** Creating Targeted Resumes

---

### 1. Learning Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Understand why a resume should be customised for each job application.
- Identify the most important requirements in a job description.
- Match a candidate's actual experience and skills to those requirements.
- Prioritise relevant information in a resume.
- Avoid generic resumes and dishonest keyword insertion.

### 2. What Does Resume Tailoring Mean?

Resume tailoring means adjusting the content, emphasis and presentation of a resume to match a particular job opportunity.

A candidate may apply for several positions using the same general background, but each employer may be looking for different skills.

**Example:**

A candidate with customer service experience may apply for:

1. Customer Service Executive
2. Sales Executive
3. Administrative Assistant

The candidate's background remains the same, but the resume should highlight different relevant strengths for each position.

| Target job | What to emphasise |
|---|---|
| Customer Service Executive | Customer communication, query handling, issue resolution |
| Sales Executive | Product presentation, customer needs, sales communication |
| Administrative Assistant | Record keeping, documentation, coordination |

**Important:** Tailoring does not mean changing the candidate's history. It means presenting genuine experience in the most relevant way.

### 3. A Simple Five-Step Resume Tailoring Process

**Step 1 – Read the Job Description**

Identify:
- Job title
- Required qualifications
- Technical or professional skills
- Experience requirements
- Main responsibilities
- Preferred tools or software

**Step 2 – Identify Important Keywords**

Look for repeated or important terms in the job description.

For example, an Office Administrator vacancy might mention:

- Microsoft Excel
- Documentation
- Scheduling
- Record management
- Email communication
- Coordination

**Step 3 – Match Requirements with Candidate Information**

Compare the job requirements with the candidate's actual qualifications, experience and skills.

| Job requirement | Candidate evidence |
|---|---|
| Documentation | Prepared letters and reports |
| Record management | Maintained office records |
| MS Excel | Used spreadsheets |
| Coordination | Coordinated meetings |

**Step 4 – Prioritise Relevant Content**

Place the most relevant information where it is easy to notice.

For example:
- Highlight relevant skills near the top.
- Give more detail to relevant work experience.
- Include relevant projects and training.
- Remove unnecessary or unrelated details where appropriate.

**Step 5 – Review the Final Resume**

Ask yourself:

- Does the resume match the target position?
- Are the important requirements addressed?
- Is every claim supported by candidate information?
- Have we avoided adding skills the candidate does not possess?

### 4. Practical Examples Using Our Four Candidates

Let's see how we can tailor each fictional candidate's resume.

**A. Aisha Rahman – Fresher**

Target job: Junior Frontend Developer

Relevant background:
- BCA graduate
- HTML, CSS and JavaScript fundamentals
- React basics
- Campus Events Portal academic project

Emphasise:
- Frontend development skills
- React project experience
- Responsive interface development
- Academic qualifications

Do not claim professional frontend development experience because she has not worked in a formal developer role.

**B. Rahul Shetty – Office Administrator**

Target job: Administrative Coordinator

Relevant background:
- Office administration experience
- Document preparation
- Record keeping
- Email and telephone enquiries
- Meeting coordination
- MS Word and Excel

Emphasise:
- Administrative support
- Documentation and records
- Office coordination
- Spreadsheet management

His file-naming improvement can be mentioned accurately, but do not invent a percentage of time saved.

**C. Naveen Kumar – Sales Executive**

Target job: Retail Sales Executive

Relevant background:
- Retail sales experience
- Customer assistance
- Product presentation
- POS billing
- Stock coordination
- Assistance to new staff

Emphasise:
- Customer engagement
- Product knowledge and presentation
- Sales communication
- Billing and retail operations

Do not claim that he achieved sales targets or increased revenue unless the candidate provides evidence.

**D. Priya Nair – HR / Recruitment Coordinator**

Target job: Recruitment Coordinator

Relevant background:
- Customer support experience
- CRM record updates
- Customer onboarding support
- Escalation coordination
- HR Fundamentals short course

Emphasise:
- Communication
- Record accuracy
- Coordination
- Onboarding support
- HR-related training

Do not describe her as an experienced recruiter because she has not held a recruitment position.

### 5. One Candidate, Two Different Job Applications

Consider Rahul Shetty.

He is applying for two positions:

**Job A – Office Administrator**

Resume headline:

*Office Administrator | Documentation | Record Management | Office Coordination*

Professional summary should highlight administrative experience, document preparation, records and office support.

**Job B – Administrative Coordinator**

Resume headline:

*Administrative Coordinator | Scheduling | Documentation | Stakeholder Coordination*

Professional summary should give greater emphasis to coordination, meeting arrangements, communication and documentation.

Notice that we have not changed his employment history or invented new skills. We have simply adjusted the emphasis to suit the position.

### 6. Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Sending exactly the same resume for every job.
2. Copying the entire job description into the resume.
3. Adding keywords without checking whether the candidate has those skills.
4. Highlighting irrelevant experience while ignoring relevant experience.
5. Changing job titles or responsibilities to make the candidate appear more experienced.
6. Removing useful information just to make the resume shorter.

**Golden Rule:** Every tailored resume must remain truthful, even when the candidate does not meet every job requirement.

### 7. Short Exercise

A company is hiring an Office Administrator.

The job description includes:

- MS Excel and Word
- Document management
- Email communication
- Meeting coordination
- Record maintenance

Which of the following candidate profiles is the closest match?

**Candidate A:** A retail sales associate experienced in customer handling and billing.

**Candidate B:** An office administrator experienced in documentation, record keeping, email communication and meeting coordination.

**Candidate C:** A fresher with HTML, CSS and React basics.

Answer these questions:

1. Which candidate is the strongest match?
2. Which keywords should be highlighted?
3. What additional information would you request from the candidate?

### 8. Today's Assignment

**Practical Task – Tailor a Resume to a Job Description**

Choose one fictional candidate from the Aeraf CV Workbook.

Find or create a simple job description matching that candidate's target role.

Prepare a short tailoring analysis containing:

1. Target job title
2. Five important job requirements
3. Candidate's matching skills and experience
4. Relevant keywords
5. Sections that should receive greater emphasis
6. Any requirements the candidate does not currently meet or has not yet verified

Then prepare a revised resume headline and professional summary based on your analysis.

**Bonus Challenge:** Use the same candidate and prepare a second headline and summary for a different but related job role.

### 9. Key Takeaways

- A good resume is tailored to a specific job opportunity.
- Understand the job description before editing the resume.
- Match job requirements with genuine candidate evidence.
- Prioritise relevant experience, skills and projects.
- Use keywords naturally and honestly.
- Never invent qualifications, experience, achievements or technical skills.
