# Aeraf CV – Resume Writer Training Programme
## Day 9 – Writing an Effective Professional Summary
**Module:** Week 2 – Resume Sections  
**Duration:** 10–15 minutes

### 🎯 Learning Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Understand the purpose of a professional summary.
- Identify the most relevant information to include.
- Write summaries for freshers, experienced professionals and career changers.
- Tailor a summary to a specific target job.
- Avoid generic statements and unsupported claims.

---

### 1. What Is a Professional Summary?

A professional summary is a short introduction near the top of a resume. It gives recruiters a quick understanding of the candidate's background, key strengths and suitability for the target role.

Think of it as a short answer to three questions:

1. Who is the candidate professionally?
2. What relevant skills, qualifications or experience do they have?
3. What value or contribution can they bring to the target role?

A good summary encourages the recruiter to continue reading the resume.

**Recommended length:**
- Fresher: Approximately 2–3 concise sentences.
- Experienced professional: Approximately 3–4 sentences.
- Career changer: Approximately 3–4 sentences, highlighting transferable skills and relevant preparation.

These are guidelines, not strict rules. Clarity and relevance matter more than word count.

### 2. Professional Summary vs Career Objective

These two sections are often confused.

| Professional Summary | Career Objective |
|---|---|
| Highlights existing qualifications, skills and experience | Explains the candidate's career direction or immediate goal |
| Focuses on what the candidate brings | Focuses on what the candidate is seeking |
| Particularly useful for experienced professionals | Can be useful for freshers or career changers |
| Should be tailored to the target job | Should also be specific and relevant |

**Generic objective – Avoid:**

"Seeking a challenging position in a reputed organisation where I can utilise my skills and grow professionally."

This statement could apply to almost anyone. It does not tell the recruiter what the candidate can offer.

**More effective approach:**

"BCA graduate with foundational knowledge of HTML, CSS and JavaScript, along with practical experience building a responsive web interface using React. Seeking a Junior Frontend Developer opportunity to apply technical skills and contribute to web development projects."

This gives the recruiter relevant information immediately.

### 3. A Simple Formula for Writing a Summary

Use the following structure:

**Professional identity + Relevant qualifications/experience + Key skills + Value or career direction**

For example:

"Office Administrator with over four years of experience supporting daily office operations, documentation and administrative coordination. Skilled in MS Office, record management, email communication and meeting coordination. Experienced in maintaining organised records and supporting smooth administrative workflows."

Notice that the summary:
- Identifies the candidate's professional role.
- Mentions relevant experience.
- Highlights skills related to the target job.
- Avoids exaggerated claims.

### 4. Practical Examples – Our Four Candidates

Let's apply the formula to the fictional profiles in our workbook.

**A. Aisha Rahman – Fresher / Junior Web Developer**

"Recent BCA graduate with foundational knowledge of HTML, CSS and JavaScript, along with basic React skills. Developed a responsive Campus Events Portal using React, featuring event listings, category filtering and event detail views. Interested in applying frontend development skills in a Junior Web Developer role while continuing to build practical experience."

Why it works:
- Highlights her education and technical skills.
- Uses her actual academic project as evidence.
- Does not claim professional employment experience.

**B. Rahul Shetty – Office Administrator**

"Office Administrator with over four years of experience supporting office operations, documentation, record management and administrative coordination. Proficient in MS Word, Excel and Outlook, with experience handling enquiries, preparing reports and coordinating meetings. Also introduced a naming convention for shared files to improve document organisation and retrieval."

Why it works:
- Establishes relevant work experience.
- Includes practical administrative skills.
- Mentions a genuine contribution without inventing a time-saving figure.

**C. Naveen Kumar – Sales Executive**

"Sales and retail professional with experience assisting customers, understanding product requirements, explaining product features and supporting order processing. Skilled in customer service, product presentation, POS billing and stock coordination. Regularly entrusted by management to assist new staff, demonstrating practical product and customer-service knowledge."

Why it works:
- Highlights relevant retail and customer-facing experience.
- Uses a supported example of trust from management.
- Does not incorrectly describe him as a formally appointed trainer or claim unverified sales figures.

**D. Priya Nair – Career Changer / HR Coordinator**

"Customer Support professional with experience handling customer enquiries, maintaining CRM records, coordinating escalations and supporting customer onboarding. Strong transferable skills in communication, documentation, scheduling and stakeholder coordination. Completed an HR Fundamentals course and seeking to transition into an entry-level HR or Recruitment Coordinator role."

Why it works:
- Connects previous experience with HR-related responsibilities.
- Highlights transferable skills.
- Mentions her relevant course without claiming professional HR experience.

### 5. Common Mistakes to Avoid

**1. Using generic statements**

Avoid phrases such as "hardworking, sincere and dedicated" without supporting evidence.

**2. Making the summary too long**

A summary should introduce the candidate, not repeat the entire resume.

**3. Repeating every skill**

Select the skills most relevant to the target job.

**4. Making unsupported claims**

Do not write "expert in React", "top-performing salesperson" or "experienced HR professional" unless the candidate's background supports those claims.

**5. Using the same summary for every job**

A candidate applying for an Office Administrator role may need to highlight documentation and coordination, while an application for an Administrative Coordinator role may place more emphasis on scheduling and stakeholder communication.

**6. Writing in the first person**

Avoid repeatedly starting sentences with "I am", "I have" or "My objective is". Use concise professional language.

### 6. Short Exercise – Improve a Weak Summary

Consider this summary:

"I am a hardworking and dedicated person looking for a good job in a reputed company. I have good communication skills and can work under pressure. I want to improve my career and learn new things."

Identify at least four weaknesses.

Now rewrite it for **Priya Nair**, applying for an entry-level HR Coordinator role. Use only information available in her workbook profile.

### 7. Small Assignment – Write Four Professional Summaries

Prepare a professional summary for each of the following candidates:

1. **Aisha Rahman:** Junior Web Developer.
2. **Rahul Shetty:** Office Administrator.
3. **Naveen Kumar:** Sales Executive.
4. **Priya Nair:** HR/Recruitment Coordinator.

Guidelines:
- Write approximately 2–4 sentences for each candidate.
- Use relevant information from the workbook.
- Tailor each summary to the target role.
- Avoid generic statements.
- Do not invent experience, qualifications, skills or achievements.

**Bonus challenge:** Write a second version of Rahul's summary targeting an Administrative Coordinator role. Notice how changing the target job changes the skills and experience you emphasise.

### Key Takeaways

- A professional summary is a short introduction to the candidate.
- Focus on qualifications, relevant skills and genuine experience.
- Tailor the summary to the target job.
- Use evidence rather than unsupported adjectives.
- Keep it concise, clear and easy to understand.
- Never exaggerate a candidate's background.
