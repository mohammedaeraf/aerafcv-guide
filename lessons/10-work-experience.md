# Aeraf CV – Resume Writer Training Programme
## Day 10 – Writing the Work Experience Section
**Module:** Week 2 – Resume Sections  
**Duration:** 10–15 minutes

### 🎯 Learning Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Present a candidate's employment history professionally.
- Format company names, job titles and employment dates consistently.
- Convert basic job responsibilities into effective resume bullet points.
- Distinguish between responsibilities and achievements.
- Handle candidates with no formal work experience.

---

### 1. What Is the Work Experience Section?

The Work Experience section describes a candidate's previous and current employment.

It helps recruiters understand:

- Where the candidate has worked.
- What roles they have performed.
- What responsibilities they handled.
- What contributions or achievements they made.
- Whether their experience is relevant to the target job.

This is one of the most important sections for experienced candidates.

For freshers, internships, academic projects, volunteering and practical training may be more relevant than formal employment history.

### 2. Standard Structure of a Work Experience Entry

Use the following structure:

**Job Title**  
Company Name | Location  
Employment Period

- Responsibility or achievement 1.
- Responsibility or achievement 2.
- Responsibility or achievement 3.

**Example:**

**Office Administrator**  
Gulf Horizon Services | Abu Dhabi, UAE  
August 2022 – Present

- Maintain office records and organise documents for easy retrieval.
- Prepare letters, reports and administrative correspondence.
- Handle email and telephone enquiries and coordinate meetings.
- Update spreadsheets and manage shared digital folders.

This format is simple, readable and easy for recruiters to understand.

### 3. How to Write Effective Resume Bullet Points

A common mistake is copying a candidate's basic job description directly into the resume.

Instead, convert the information into clear, action-oriented bullet points.

Use this simple formula:

**Action Verb + Task or Responsibility + Context or Result (where supported)**

Examples:

| Basic Statement | Improved Resume Bullet |
|---|---|
| Responsible for office files | Maintained and organised office records and digital files for easy retrieval. |
| Answered phone calls | Handled telephone and email enquiries and directed requests appropriately. |
| Helped customers | Assisted customers in understanding product features and selecting suitable products. |
| Updated computer records | Updated CRM records to maintain accurate customer information. |
| Helped new employees | Assisted new staff members in understanding product information and customer-service procedures. |

Notice that the improved versions use professional language while retaining the original meaning.

**Important:** Improving the language does not mean exaggerating the candidate's responsibilities.

### 4. Choosing Strong Action Verbs

Start resume bullet points with meaningful action verbs.

| Area | Useful Action Verbs |
|---|---|
| Administration | Maintained, Organised, Prepared, Coordinated, Updated |
| Sales | Assisted, Presented, Recommended, Processed, Supported |
| Customer Service | Responded, Resolved, Guided, Communicated, Handled |
| Technical | Developed, Designed, Implemented, Tested, Configured |
| Management | Led, Supervised, Managed, Monitored, Coordinated |

Avoid starting every bullet point with the same word, such as "Responsible for" or "Worked on".

Choose verbs that accurately describe what the candidate actually did.

### 5. Responsibilities vs Achievements

These are related but different.

**Responsibilities** describe the regular duties performed by a candidate.

Example:
- Prepared administrative reports and maintained office records.

**Achievements** describe a contribution, improvement or result.

Example:
- Introduced a consistent file-naming convention that made shared documents easier to locate.

Achievements can make a resume more compelling because they show how the candidate contributed beyond routine duties.

However, never invent achievements or numerical results.

If the candidate says they improved efficiency by 30%, ask how that figure was measured before including it.

### 7. What About Freshers?

Aisha Rahman does not have formal employment experience.

Do not create a fake employment section or describe her academic project as professional employment.

Instead, use a relevant section such as:

**Academic Project – Campus Events Portal**

- Developed a responsive event-listing interface using React.
- Implemented category filtering to help users browse events.
- Created event detail views using sample data.
- Applied frontend development concepts to build an interactive user interface.

This allows Aisha to demonstrate practical skills without claiming work experience she does not have.

### 8. Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Listing employment in random order instead of reverse-chronological order.
2. Using inconsistent date formats.
3. Writing long paragraphs instead of concise bullet points.
4. Repeating the same responsibilities under every job.
5. Using vague statements such as "Did office work".
6. Inventing achievements, performance figures or job responsibilities.
7. Including confidential company or customer information.
8. Changing official job titles without the candidate's confirmation.

**Resume Writer Tip:** If a candidate has employment gaps, do not automatically hide or manipulate dates. Ask for clarification and present the history honestly.

### 10. Small Assignment – Prepare Work Experience Sections

Using the workbook profiles, prepare the Work Experience section for:

1. **Rahul Shetty:** Include both employment positions.
2. **Naveen Kumar:** Include both employment positions.
3. **Priya Nair:** Include her Customer Support Executive position.
4. **Aisha Rahman:** Prepare an Academic Project section instead of a Work Experience section.

Guidelines:

- Use reverse-chronological order.
- Include job title, company, location and dates.
- Write 3–5 relevant bullet points per position where information supports them.
- Use appropriate action verbs.
- Separate responsibilities from genuine achievements.
- Do not invent numbers, results or responsibilities.

### Key Takeaways

- The Work Experience section explains a candidate's professional background.
- Present the most recent employment first.
- Use clear job titles, company names, locations and dates.
- Begin bullet points with relevant action verbs.
- Highlight genuine achievements wherever evidence is available.
- Freshers can demonstrate practical experience through academic projects, internships and training.
- Accuracy is more important than making a resume sound impressive.
