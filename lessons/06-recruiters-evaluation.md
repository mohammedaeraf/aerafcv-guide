## 2. Who Reads a Resume?

A resume may be reviewed by different people and systems during the recruitment process.

Understanding their expectations helps us prepare better resumes.

There are three important audiences.

### A. Recruiters and HR Teams

Recruiters are often the first people to review applications.

They typically look for:

- Is the candidate suitable for the advertised position?
- Does the candidate have the required qualifications?
- Is the relevant experience clearly presented?
- Are the important skills easy to identify?
- Does the resume contain any obvious inconsistencies?

Recruiters may receive hundreds of applications for a vacancy. Therefore, clarity and relevance are extremely important.

**Our responsibility:** Make relevant information easy to find without forcing the recruiter to read unnecessary details.

### B. Hiring Managers

Hiring managers are usually responsible for evaluating whether a candidate can perform the actual job.

They may look more closely at:

- Relevant professional experience.
- Technical or functional knowledge.
- Previous responsibilities.
- Projects and achievements.
- Evidence of problem-solving abilities.
- Experience with relevant tools and processes.

For example, a hiring manager recruiting a Junior Web Developer may want to understand whether the candidate has actually worked with HTML, CSS, JavaScript and React.

Simply listing these technologies is not always sufficient. Supporting project evidence makes the resume stronger.

**Our responsibility:** Present skills with relevant examples wherever possible.

### C. Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS)

An Applicant Tracking System is software used by employers to manage recruitment applications.

Depending on the system and employer configuration, an ATS may:

- Store candidate resumes.
- Extract information such as names, contact details and employment history.
- Organise applications.
- Search for relevant skills and keywords.
- Support recruiters in managing candidate applications.

An ATS is not necessarily an automatic rejection machine. Different systems and recruitment processes work differently.

**Our responsibility:** Prepare resumes with clear headings, readable text and relevant terminology so that information can be understood by both people and recruitment software.

## 3. What Makes a Resume Effective?

A good resume should satisfy five important requirements.

### 1. Relevance

The resume should focus on information related to the target job.

For example:

If Aisha applies for a Junior Web Developer position, her React project is more relevant than unrelated personal activities.

### 2. Clarity

Information should be written in simple, professional language.

Avoid unnecessarily complicated sentences.

**Weak example:**

"Responsible for undertaking various kinds of activities associated with the maintenance and management of different office-related documentation."

**Better example:**

"Maintained office records, prepared reports and organised administrative documents."

### 3. Evidence

A resume should provide evidence of the candidate's capabilities.

**Weak example:**

"Good at customer service."

**Better example:**

"Assisted customers with product selection, explained product features and processed orders."

The second example explains what the candidate actually did.

### 4. Readability

A recruiter should be able to locate important information easily.

Use:
- Clear section headings.
- Consistent formatting.
- Readable fonts.
- Appropriate spacing.
- Short bullet points.
- Logical section ordering.

### 5. Accuracy

Every statement must be supported by genuine candidate information.

Never add qualifications, employment history, achievements or technical skills simply because they appear desirable.

## 4. Practical Example – Evaluating Aisha's Resume

Let's use Profile A – Aisha Rahman from our training workbook.

**Target Position:** Junior Web Developer

Suppose we prepare the following two versions of her project description.

### Version A – Poor Presentation

"Worked on a college project. Used some programming languages and developed a website. Interested in learning new technologies."

### Version B – Professional Presentation

**Campus Events Portal**

- Built a responsive event-listing interface using React and sample data.
- Implemented category-based filtering.
- Added event detail views to help users explore campus events.

### How Would Different Readers Evaluate Them?

| Reader | Version A | Version B |
|---|---|---|
| Recruiter | Project details are unclear | Relevant development experience is visible |
| Hiring Manager | Cannot understand what was developed | Can identify React usage and implemented features |
| ATS | Limited role-specific terminology | Contains relevant technical terms in readable text |

Version B is stronger because it communicates specific project work.

Notice that we have not invented any additional technical capabilities or project outcomes.

## 5. Common Resume Problems Recruiters May Notice

As resume writers, we should be able to identify the following problems.

| Problem | Why It Is a Concern |
|---|---|
| Generic career objective | Does not communicate relevance |
| Missing employment dates | Makes career history difficult to understand |
| Spelling and grammatical mistakes | Reduces professionalism |
| Long paragraphs | Makes information difficult to scan |
| Irrelevant personal information | Distracts from professional qualifications |
| Unsupported achievements | Creates credibility concerns |
| Inconsistent formatting | Makes the document look unprofessional |
| Unclear job titles | Makes professional experience difficult to evaluate |

A resume should be concise, but conciseness should never come at the cost of important information.

## 8. Key Takeaways

- Recruiters, hiring managers and ATS platforms have different roles in recruitment.
- Recruiters generally look for relevance and suitability.
- Hiring managers look for evidence of actual job-related capabilities.
- ATS platforms help employers manage and process applications.
- Clear headings, readable text and logical formatting improve usability.
- Specific responsibilities and project details are stronger than vague claims.
- Accuracy and relevance are more important than impressive-sounding language.

---

### AERAF CV – PROFESSIONAL WRITING PRINCIPLE

**"Write for the recruiter. Provide evidence for the hiring manager. Structure for readability."**

Our objective is not simply to make a resume look attractive. It is to make the candidate's genuine professional value easy to understand and evaluate.
