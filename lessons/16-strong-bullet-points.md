# Aeraf CV – Resume Writer Training Programme

## DAY 16 – WRITING STRONG AND IMPACTFUL RESUME BULLET POINTS

**Week:** 3 – Professional Resume Writing  
**Duration:** 10–15 minutes

### 1. Learning Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
- Write clear and professional resume bullet points.
- Replace weak descriptions with stronger statements.
- Use action verbs effectively.
- Present responsibilities in a concise and meaningful way.

### 2. Why Are Resume Bullet Points Important?

Recruiters usually scan resumes rather than reading every sentence carefully.

Good bullet points help recruiters quickly understand:
- What the candidate did.
- What skills they used.
- What responsibilities they handled.
- What value they contributed.

Compare these examples:

**Weak:**
- Responsible for handling customers.
- Worked on office documents.
- Helped with sales.

**Improved:**
- Assisted customers with product selection and enquiries.
- Prepared office letters, reports and administrative documents.
- Supported retail sales through product presentation and customer assistance.

Notice how the improved versions communicate the work more clearly.

### 3. A Simple Formula for Writing Bullet Points

Use this formula:

**Action Verb + Task + Context / Method + Result (where supported)**

Example:

"Maintained office records using spreadsheets and organised shared folders for easier document retrieval."

Breakdown:
- Action verb: Maintained
- Task: Office records
- Method: Spreadsheets and organised shared folders
- Result: Easier document retrieval

Not every bullet needs a measurable result. Never invent results just to make a statement impressive.

### 5. Use Strong Action Verbs

Choose verbs that accurately describe the candidate's actual contribution.

| Area | Action verbs |
|---|---|
| Administration | Maintained, Organised, Prepared, Coordinated |
| Sales | Assisted, Presented, Processed, Recommended |
| Customer Service | Responded, Resolved, Supported, Communicated |
| Technical | Developed, Designed, Implemented, Tested |
| Management | Led, Managed, Supervised, Directed |

**Important:** Use words such as Led, Managed and Supervised only when the candidate genuinely performed those responsibilities.

### 6. Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Starting every bullet with "Responsible for".
2. Writing long paragraphs instead of concise bullet points.
3. Repeating the same action verbs unnecessarily.
4. Using vague statements such as "Did office work".
5. Including unsupported achievements or performance figures.
6. Using complicated words that make the resume difficult to understand.

Aim for one clear idea per bullet point. Usually, 3–5 relevant bullets per position are sufficient, depending on the role and experience.

### 7. Short Exercise

Improve these three weak bullet points:

1. Worked in a sales shop.
2. Did computer work and maintained files.
3. Helped customers with their problems.

Try rewriting them using strong action verbs and specific descriptions.

### 8. Today's Assignment

Select one candidate from our Aeraf CV Workbook.

Rewrite at least **five weak or basic job responsibility statements** into professional resume bullet points.

Requirements:
- Start each bullet with an appropriate action verb.
- Clearly describe the task.
- Include relevant tools or methods where known.
- Add results only when supported by actual information.
- Avoid exaggerating the candidate's responsibilities.

**Bonus Challenge:** Ask another trainee to review your five bullet points and suggest improvements.

### 9. Key Takeaways

- Strong bullet points communicate responsibilities clearly.
- Start with relevant action verbs.
- Be specific and concise.
- Highlight relevant skills and contributions.
- Use measurable results only when verified.
- Never exaggerate a candidate's achievements.
